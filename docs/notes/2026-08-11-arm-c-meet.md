# Arm C-meet — composing the negotiated contract with the mandate

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Closes outline G9.** Implements the arm the arm C note argued for.
**Data:** `arm_c_meet_v1..v5` on `data/bargain_3_9`, γ = 0.4, T_max = 6, 5 seeds.
**Model:** `gemini-2.5-flash`, `LLM_REASONING_EFFORT=minimal`.

## Reproduce

```bash
cd ../multi-agent-marketplace && docker compose up -d && source .env
cd ../self-negotiated-contracts
for i in 1 2 3 4 5; do
  uv run python experiments/arm_c_meet.py --data data/bargain_3_9 \
      --gamma 0.4 --t-max 6 --experiment "arm_c_meet_v$i" --override
done
uv run pytest tests/test_contract_algebra.py -q     # the algebra it rests on
```

---

## What the arm is

Arm C established that a self-negotiated θ is enforced as reliably as an imposed
one but does **not** deliver the mandate: no envelope refined the platform's
contract, budgets ran 1.05–1.52× above it on all 9 pairs, and arm C settled a
higher fraction of breaching deals than doing nothing. The fix it named is
composition. Here the enforced contract is

    θ_enforced = θ_negotiated ∧ θ_mandate

the meet in the refinement lattice — componentwise `min` on upper bounds, `max`
on lower bounds, with C(a ∧ b) = C(a) ∩ C(b) exactly (formulation §9.5,
Proposition 3). The pre-phase is identical to arm C; only the contract handed to
the filter differs, so arms C and C-meet differ in exactly one thing.

## Predictions, registered before the runs

Recorded in the plan file before any arm C-meet run existed, and reproduced here
verbatim so they cannot be retrofitted:

1. **The meet refines the mandate by construction, so settled breaches against
   the scenario θ should be 0** — recovering arm B's guarantee.
2. **The meet also refines the negotiated envelope**, so whatever the parties
   agreed is preserved inside it.
3. **The meet is tighter than either input**, so the intervention rate should be
   at least arm B's and the funnel point may move.

A further prior was recorded at the same time, from computing the meet on the
stored envelopes without running anything: **the funnel point would not move.**
The buyer states its budget verbatim as its counter-offer, so
c_negotiated = B_mandate / q_min *exactly*, the meet's safe set collapses to a
single admissible price, and that price is arm B's funnel point B/q_min.

## What the composition looks like before any agent runs

Computed from the stored arm C envelopes, no live runs involved:

| pair | B_meet | c_meet | c·q_min | margin | tighter than mandate on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `business_0001\|customer_0001` | 11.58 | 5.79 | 11.58 | **0.00** | cost_floor |
| `business_0002\|customer_0001` | 11.58 | 5.79 | 11.58 | **0.00** | cost_floor |
| `business_0004\|customer_0002` | 7.45 | 7.45 | 7.45 | **0.00** | cost_floor |
| `business_0005\|customer_0002` | 7.45 | 7.45 | 7.45 | **0.00** | cost_floor |
| `business_0008\|customer_0003` | 31.38 | 10.46 | 31.38 | **0.00** | cost_floor |
| `business_0003`, `0006`, `0007`, `0009` | = mandate | | | < 0 | none — already unsatisfiable |

So the negotiated side **bites on 5 of 9 pairs, always on the cost floor and
never on the budget**, and on every one of those five the safe set is degenerate:
a single admissible price. On the remaining four the meet is the mandate
unchanged, and those are exactly the four pairs whose mandate was already
unsatisfiable.

That degeneracy is a property of *this* scenario, not of composition. It follows
from `bargain_3_9` telling the buyer its budget, so the buyer's revealed floor
coincides with the platform's ceiling. On a scenario where the buyer's stated
price sat strictly below the mandate's ceiling the meet would have interior.

## Results

### The five arms

| | arm A | arm D | arm B | arm C | **arm C-meet** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| proposals offered | 95 | 80 | 51 | 219 | 67 |
| breaching the **scenario** θ | 0.858 | 0.822 | 0.472 | 0.922 | 0.664 |
| deals settled | 12 | 12 | 16 | 14 | **15** |
| settled breaching the scenario θ | 0.083 | 0.083 | **0.000** | 0.143 | **0.000** |
| meaningful breaches | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | **0** |
| overspend | £0.00 | £0.00 | £0.00 | £0.48 | **£0.00** |
| value transacted | £143.75 | £156.27 | £259.45 | £220.69 | £251.60 |

### The predictions, scored

**1 — the meet refines the mandate, so settled breaches should be 0. HOLDS.**
0 of 15 settled deals breach the scenario θ; overspend is £0.00. Arm C settled
2 of 14 and overspent £0.48. Composition recovers arm B's guarantee **while
preserving closure** — 15 deals against arm B's 16 and arm A's 12 — so the
guarantee is not bought by suppressing trade.

The structural half is exact rather than statistical: **the enforced contract
refines the mandate in 27 of 27 frozen instances**, which it must, by
Proposition 3.

**2 — the meet also refines the negotiated envelope. HOLDS**, 27 of 27. What the
parties agreed is preserved inside what is enforced; the platform removes
possibilities the mandate already forbade and nothing else.

**3 — the meet is tighter, so the intervention rate should be at least arm B's
and the funnel point may move. SPLIT: the first half fails, the second was
already answered.**

| | arm B | arm C-meet |
|---|---|---|
| corrected / **enforced** rounds | 24/27 = **0.89** | 14/17 = **0.82** |

The correction rate is *lower*, not higher, so the prediction as written is
**not confirmed**. The reason is that arm C-meet does less work per negotiation
rather than less work per round: its enforced phase is 17 rounds against arm B's
27, because the pre-phase has already absorbed the opening exchange that arm B
spends most of its corrections on. Tighter contracts do not imply more
intervention when the tighter contract governs a shorter window.

And the funnel point does **not** move, exactly as the pre-registered prior said:

| arm | mean per-pair settled-price SD | funnel point |
|---|---|---|
| B | 0.000 | B/q_min |
| C | 0.090 | B/q_min, loosely |
| **C-meet** | **0.028** | **B/q_min** |

Every arm C-meet settled price is B/q_min to the cent except one (5.565 on
`business_0001|customer_0001`), and that one is the seed discussed below where
the meet was unsatisfiable and the pair therefore ran unfiltered.

### Does the negotiated side actually bite?

**Yes, on 17 of 27 frozen instances, spread over 4 distinct pairs — and always
on the cost floor, never on the budget.**

| pair | seeds where it bites | of which degenerate |
|---|---|---|
| `business_0001\|customer_0001` | 5 | 4 |
| `business_0002\|customer_0001` | 4 | 4 |
| `business_0004\|customer_0002` | 3 | 3 |
| `business_0008\|customer_0003` | 5 | 5 |

So the composition is not a formality that collapses to the mandate: on the
majority of governed pairs the agents' own agreement genuinely narrows what the
platform would have allowed. But **16 of those 17 are degenerate** — the safe set
is a single price — for the scenario-specific reason set out above, and the
direction of the bite is one-sided. The negotiated contract only ever raised the
floor; it never lowered the ceiling, because the seller's opening ask is above
the mandate's budget on every pair.

### The pre-phase still resolves, and composition can destroy feasibility

| | |
|---|---|
| pairs where θ was agreed | **27/37 (0.73)** |
| meets that are unsatisfiable | 11/27 |
| — because the *mandate* already was | 10 |
| — **because composition destroyed feasibility** | **1** |

The freeze rate matches arm C's 0.72, so composing changes nothing about
whether the parties can agree — it operates after they have.

The single case where composition destroyed feasibility is worth stating
plainly, because it is the cost of the operation. On one seed of
`business_0001|customer_0001` the buyer counter-offered **above its own stated
budget** (6.365/unit, £12.73 against a budget of £11.58), so c_negotiated
exceeded B_mandate/q_min and the meet had no common ground. The pair ran
unfiltered, as any unsatisfiable contract does (limitation B4), and produced the
one breaching enforced round in the whole arm. Excluding it:

> **On satisfiable meets: 16 enforced rounds, 0 breaching.**

That is the honest form of the safety claim for this arm. The composition does
not fail there; it reports that the parties agreed something the mandate
forbids entirely, and declines to project into an empty set. Whether the
platform should then *refuse the trade* rather than merely decline to filter is
open question G8, and this arm makes it more pressing rather than less.

## Two bugs, and one near-miss finding

Neither is incidental; both are in chapter 4 as methodological material.

**The negotiated arms raised on every governed round (bug 6).** The freeze-time
projection referenced a `PairState` field a botched edit never added, so every
proposal after θ was agreed raised `AttributeError`. The whole suite passed —
every negotiated-arm test stopped at the freeze, so the pre-phase was covered
and the enforced phase never was.

The live symptom looks nothing like a crash. The exception escapes
`execute_action` *before* the action row is persisted, so the message never
arrives, nothing is logged as an error, and both agents poll until they hit
their step limit. **Five seeds ran to completion, wrote plausible certificates,
and recorded 0 enforced rounds out of 35** — which reads as a finding ("the
pre-phase consumes everything, so a negotiated contract never binds") and which
I came close to writing up as one.

What caught it was a control, not the logs. An arm B run made at that moment
looked normal (222 actions, 72 sends) while an arm C run made at the same moment
looked broken (1824 actions, 67 sends, against 666/187 stored). Arm B's code
path was untouched by the diff and arm C's was not, which ruled out the
"the API is being throttled" diagnosis I had already half-accepted.

**The projection returned the breach on a degenerate safe set (bug 7).** With
C(θ) a single point, the projection step's feasible region is that point written
as two opposing inequalities; OSQP returns `maximum iterations reached` with
u = 0, and `project_into_safe_set` handed back the *unsafe* input while
reporting success. Arm C-meet breached 2 of 6 enforced rounds this way. Closed
with a closed-form feasibility fallback — guaranteed inside C(θ) when C(θ) is
non-empty, not claimed to be the metric-nearest point (limitation B7).

## Limitations

- **The degeneracy is scenario-specific.** `bargain_3_9` tells the buyer its
  budget, so the buyer's revealed floor coincides with the platform's ceiling
  and the meet collapses to a point on every pair where it bites. On a scenario
  where the buyer's stated price sat strictly below the ceiling the meet would
  have interior, and the funnel, intervention and degeneracy results would all
  differ. **[2026-08-21: `undisclosed_3_9` has now been run and does not answer
  this. The pre-phase never closes there -- freeze rate 0.000 across 10 runs,
  because an uninformed buyer names no price -- so no meet is formed at all.
  The degeneracy question needs a scenario where the buyer states a price that
  is disclosed but *not* pinned to the mandate's ceiling. See
  `2026-08-21-composition-undisclosed.md`.]**
- **n = 5 seeds × 3 customers.** The 0/15 settled-breach result is categorical.
  The correction-rate comparison (0.82 against 0.89) is 17 rounds against 27 and
  should not be read as a rate difference.
- **The bite is one-sided.** The negotiated contract raised the cost floor and
  never lowered the budget, so "the negotiated side bites" is really "the buyer's
  revealed price binds". A scenario where a seller's ask fell below the mandate's
  ceiling would test the other direction and is not present here.
- **Arm C's comparison numbers predate two protocol fixes.** Neither changes the
  terms arm C delivers — its envelope contains the seller's opening ask by
  construction, so the freeze-time projection is a provable no-op there, and its
  envelope is never degenerate — but arm C's opening/continuation bookkeeping
  would be drawn differently if re-run. Arm C was not re-run, because doing so
  would add a fresh draw of run-to-run variance to remove a difference that is
  provably nil in the delivered terms.
- **Enforced-round counts are small.** 17 rounds across five seeds, because the
  pre-phase absorbs the opening and the degenerate contract ends the negotiation
  immediately afterwards. The safety claim rests on the settled outcomes, which
  are measured from the database, not on the round count.
